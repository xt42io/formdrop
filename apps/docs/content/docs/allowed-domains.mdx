---
title: Allowed domains
description: Restricting which sites can post to a form, and what that does and does not protect.
---

By default a form accepts a submission from anywhere. That is what makes the
plain HTML case work: a browser posts from your site, and there is nothing to
configure.

Adding an allowlist in **Settings → Allowed domains** restricts it to the
hosts you name.

## How entries match

| Entry | Matches | Does not match |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `example.com` | `example.com` | `www.example.com`, `example.com.attacker.test` |
| `*.example.com` | `example.com`, `www.example.com`, `app.example.com` | `notexample.com` |

An entry is matched against the **hostname**, so scheme and port are ignored
and case does not matter.

<Callout title="A bare domain does not include subdomains">
  `example.com` matches that host and nothing else. If your site is served
  from `www.example.com`, list `*.example.com` — or both.
</Callout>

## Requests with no origin

A browser sends an `Origin` header on a cross-origin form post, and that is
what the allowlist is checked against.

**When a form has an allowlist and a request arrives with no origin at all,
it is refused.** That is deliberate: the alternative is that anything not
sending the header walks straight past the list, which makes the list
decorative.

The consequence is worth knowing before you turn it on: a `curl` command, a
server-to-server post, or a mobile app that does not set an `Origin` will
start getting `403`. If you post from your own backend, either leave the
allowlist empty or do the restricting there.

## What this protects against

It stops your form endpoint being embedded on a site you do not control —
somebody copying your HTML onto their own page and pointing the submissions
at your dashboard.

## What it does not

<Callout type="warn" title="This is not authentication">
  The `Origin` header is set by the browser, honestly, because a browser
  enforces it. A script is not a browser: it can send whatever origin it
  likes, or none. An allowlist raises the effort required; it does not make
  the endpoint private.
</Callout>

If a form must only ever be submitted by something you trust, put it behind
your own server and post from there — where you can require a session, check
a CSRF token, or apply whatever rule actually matters to you.

## Turning it off

Remove every entry. An empty allowlist means allow all, which is the default
a new form starts with.
