FormDrop separates the two kinds of connection, because they answer different
questions:

- **Notifications** tell a person something arrived.
- **Integrations** put the data somewhere else.

Both are configured per form, in the tabs of the same name.

### Recipients confirm their own address

Adding an email recipient sends them a verification link, and nothing is
delivered to that address until they follow it. That is deliberate: without
it, anyone could point somebody else's inbox at a busy form.

The form's owner is always a recipient when email is on, and does not need to
confirm — their address is already verified from signing up.

## What happens when a delivery fails

A notification is not sent inside the request that collects the submission.
The submission is stored first and the delivery is queued alongside it, in the
same database transaction — so a provider having a bad afternoon delays the
notification and can never lose the submission.

Failed deliveries are retried with a widening gap, roughly over half an hour.
Something that never succeeds is kept and marked failed rather than
disappearing quietly, so a notification that never arrived is a thing you can
find out about.

Each destination is queued separately. One bounced email address does not
hold up Slack, and it does not stop the other four recipients.
