A submission is stored first and delivered afterwards. That ordering is the
whole design, and it is why a provider having a bad afternoon delays a
notification rather than losing a form fill.

## The channels

| Channel | Where it goes | Configure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Email** | The form's owner, plus any verified recipients | Notifications tab |
| **Slack** | A channel, via an incoming webhook | Notifications tab |
| **Discord** | A server channel, via a webhook | Notifications tab |
| **Google Sheets** | A row appended to a sheet | Integrations tab |

Each destination is queued separately. One bounced address does not hold up
Slack, and it does not stop the other recipients.

## Email recipients verify themselves

Adding a recipient sends them a confirmation link, and nothing is delivered
to that address until they follow it.

Without that, anyone could point somebody else's inbox at a busy form. The
owner is always a recipient when email is on and does not confirm separately —
their address was verified at signup.

## What happens when a delivery fails

The submission and the intent to deliver it are written in the **same
database transaction**, so there is no window where a submission exists with
nothing queued to tell you about it.

A worker then drains the queue:

1. A failed delivery is retried with a widening gap — roughly 30 seconds,
   then 2 minutes, 5, 15, and 30.
2. Six attempts, spanning about half an hour. That covers the case this is
   for: a provider having a bad few minutes.
3. Anything still failing is marked failed and **kept**, with the error that
   stopped it — rather than disappearing quietly.

The point of the last one is that a notification which never arrived is
something you can find out about, instead of a silence you have to notice.

<Callout title="A failed notification is not a lost submission">
  Delivery and collection are separate. If Slack was down when a form was
  filled in, the submission is in your dashboard regardless.
</Callout>

## Webhooks

<Callout type="info" title="Coming soon">
  Outbound webhooks — a JSON payload to any URL you choose — are designed and
  not yet available. The channel exists in the delivery queue, so when they
  land they inherit the same retry and failure handling as everything above.
</Callout>

Until then, the way to get submissions into your own system is to
[read them from the API](/sdks/javascript), or to post from your own server
so you already have the data before it reaches us.
